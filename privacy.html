Privacy

Bottlefolio · last updated 2 November 2026

Bottlefolio is a whiskey collection app made by one person. It has no advertising, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party advertising identifiers. Nothing about you is sold, rented or shared for marketing, and nothing is used to build a profile of you anywhere but in your own copy of the app.

This page says exactly what the app stores, where it goes, who can see it, and how to delete all of it. Everything below is a description of what the code does.

What the app stores about you

You can use most of the app without an account. If you sign in, you sign in with Google, and Google tells the app three things: your email address, your display name, and an account identifier. The email address is used to recognise you when you come back. The display name is what other people see if you share your shelf with them.

Everything you enter is kept under your own account identifier in a Google Firebase Realtime Database:

  • the bottles you own, and what you have recorded about them — price, proof, where you bought it, how full it is;
  • your wishlist;
  • your tasting notes and your pour log, including the place name you choose when you log a pour;
  • your flights, favourites and taste profile;
  • your settings;
  • the end of the app's own diagnostic log, so that another of your devices can read it when something goes wrong.

A copy is also kept in your browser's own storage on each device, so the app works with no signal. That copy is on your device and goes nowhere.

Who else can see it

By default, nobody. Your shelf is readable only by you. Three things can change that, and each one is something you do on purpose:

  • Sharing your shelf with somebody. You name a person, they accept, and from then on they can read your shelf until you stop sharing. Either of you can end it.
  • The directory. So that you can find somebody to share with, every signed-in account appears in a list holding a display name and an account identifier, and nothing else — no email address, no shelf, no notes. Any signed-in person can read that list.
  • A gift link. If you make one, anyone holding the link can open it without an account, which is the point of it. It carries your display name, your wishlist by name, your taste profile, and your owned bottles as one-way hashes rather than names. You can switch a link off at any time, and each link is capped in how much it may do per day.

One link leaves the app rather than calling out from it: a bottle's buy link opens a Google search for that bottle's name in your browser, the same as typing it yourself. Nothing of yours goes with it but the name you were looking at.

Location

Your position is never stored, and never leaves at full precision. It is read once, at the moment you tap to find where you are, and never in the background.

Your device can report a position accurate to a few centimetres. The app rounds it to four decimal places — about eleven metres, roughly a building — before anything leaves, because the question being asked is only “what is within a hundred and fifty metres of here”.

The rounded position goes to a public OpenStreetMap mirror, which answers with the names of nearby places. Nothing keeps the position: if you log a pour, what is saved is the place name you tapped, the same way a proof is saved — never a coordinate.

The Google Sheet switch

Settings › Shelf carries Keep a Google Sheet of my shelf, and it is worth being exact about where that sheet goes. It is created by the lookup service, so it lives in the Google Drive of the account that service runs as — the author's — and not in yours. One account owns it. If it is already somebody else's, your shelf is sent once, refused, and the switch turns itself back off; nothing is written and nothing is kept. Leave it off and no copy is made at all.

Photographs

When you photograph a bottle, a label or your shelf, the picture is sent to the app's own service to be read, and the text that comes back is what is kept — the name, the proof, the age. The photograph itself is not stored by the app, not stored by the service, and not added to any training data.

Children

Bottlefolio is about whiskey and is intended for adults of legal drinking age in their country. It is not directed at children and is not designed to collect anything from them.

Deleting everything

In the app: Settings › Account › Delete account. It is immediate and it is not recoverable.

It removes your shelf, wishlist, flights, notes, pour log, taste profile, settings and stored log; your entry in the directory; every share you granted and every share granted to you; any request you made or received; and your Google sign-in for this app. Your device's local copy is wiped last, once the rest has gone.

Gift links you published are deleted with the account. If you want one gone sooner, switch it off or delete it from the link's own row.

If you cannot reach the app, send the request through Help › Show me the log › Tell me what you think from any device still signed in, or open an issue at github.com/bzrimsek/Bottlefolio/issues. Say which account it is and it will be removed by hand.

One thing survives on purpose, and it is not about you: if the shared whiskey library has accepted a correction you offered — that a bottling is 100 proof, say — that fact stays in the library, because it is a fact about a whiskey rather than about you, and other people's shelves now rest on it. It carries no name, no account and no link back to you.

How long things are kept

Your data stays until you delete it. There is no expiry and no archive; deleting is the only thing that removes it.

Security

Every read and write is checked by rules on the database itself rather than by the app, so a modified copy of the app cannot reach another person's shelf. Traffic is over HTTPS. A gift link is the only thing in the database readable without a sign-in, and only by its own unguessable token — everything else, the shared whiskey library included, refuses a reader with no account. A link whose owner has deleted their account stops answering, and the links themselves are deleted with the account.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it and the new version replaces this page. The app's changelog records what changed and when.

Contact
